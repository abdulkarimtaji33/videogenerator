## 1. Bottom line

**Format first.** Across about 250 measured channels, niches split by format. Explainers, documentary, mystery and horror narration win in **long-form** and are weak in Shorts. Cute or character-driven AI clips win in **Shorts** and are weak in long-form. A few large, polished channels win in both. YouTube itself says viewers rarely follow a creator across formats, so treat long-form and Shorts as **separate products**.

**Money first.** Shorts earn no ad revenue below 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days (from Feb 1, 2027), and new applicants need 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views. **Long-form is the route to monetization.** Shorts are reach experiments.

**English long-form candidates, with the trade-off stated plainly:**

| Pick | Why | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| History documentary (15-35 min) | Small-channel proof exists (History Hustle 318K subs, 48K median); AI clips fit atmosphere and reenactment | Needs sourced scripts, maps and archival art; crowded; avoid "sleep history" templates |
| Mysteries, folklore, horror narration (15-45 min) | Best small-channel proof of any niche (Ghost Theory 219K subs, 152K median; MysteryTV 161K subs, 38K median) | True crime about real people is a policy and defamation risk |
| Space and science explainers (10-25 min) | Best match to AI visuals, low policy risk, mid RPM | **No small-channel proof found**; all winners are large |

The first research pass ranked science and space first, by visual fit and policy risk. The format study found stronger small-channel evidence for history and mysteries. **Choose by whether you value proof (history or mysteries) or visual fit (science), or run a short pilot of two.** Details are in section 5.

**Urdu/Hindi:** the only verified small-scale proof is long-form Urdu history (Urdu Diary, 1.21M subs, 46.5K median views) and horror narration (Urdu Corner, Hindi Horror Stories). Per-view income is much lower than English. Treat it as a second channel for later.

**Money per view and competition (rough, low confidence):** English long-form is mid-range at about $6-15 RPM with high but fragmented competition. Audience-market RPM for Urdu/Hindi is far lower (about $0.10-0.38 for a Pakistan audience, about Rs 15-170 per 1,000 views for an India audience), Hindi is saturated and Urdu long-form is thinner. **Why English first:** at those rates, getting through monetization and then earning anything is much harder in Urdu/Hindi, and no verified successful faceless Urdu or Hindi channel in the history or science niches turned up.

**Where you get paid matters:** YPP eligibility and payout depend on the country of the AdSense account holder, which is your choice of company or residency, not something assumed here.

**Evidence quality:** most numbers are measured from public channel data (views and subscribers) and are solid for what they are. Revenue, retention, and which channels use AI visuals are not visible and are marked unverified. No verified new AI-visual channel with strong views turned up in any niche, so any pilot is an experiment.

## 2. How to read the evidence

- **Measured** numbers come from a script that reads public YouTube channel pages on 2026-11-21: subscribers, the median and maximum views of the latest 20 long-form uploads, and the median and maximum views of the latest ~30 Shorts. About 250 distinct channels were tried; roughly 40% of guessed handles did not exist, so the sample is whatever could be found and measured.
- **Cross-check:** at least eight channels measured by the research passes and by me gave identical numbers. That confirms the tool is consistent; it does not confirm every other number.
- **Ratio** = Shorts median divided by subscribers. A high ratio means Shorts reach beyond the existing audience.
- **Survivorship bias:** the channels that could be found are mostly large and successful. Evidence on small or new channels is thin; each niche says so.
- **Format (AI-made vs human-made)** could not be measured. Most labels are the researchers' reading of the channel and are unknown unless a source is named.
- Revenue and average view duration are not visible. Watch-hour and RPM figures are arithmetic on stated assumptions or blog estimates, and RPM sources conflict widely.
- Policy statements were checked against YouTube's own pages where possible; see section 8.

## 3. Rules that apply to every niche

### Monetization thresholds
- From **Feb 1, 2027**, new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus **8,000 public watch hours** (last 365 days) or **20M Shorts views** (last 90 days). Today it is 4,000 hours or 10M Shorts views. Existing partners are exempt. Confidence: high (several outlets agree); the YouTube page itself was not read.
- A brand-new channel will not reach 4,000 hours in the roughly 10 weeks left, so plan for the **8,000-hour** bar.
- At a 5-minute average view, 8,000 hours is about **96,000 long-form views** (about 120,000 at 4 minutes).
- Shorts are a poor revenue path. Confirmed on YouTube's own blog: from Feb 1, 2027 a channel needs 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days to earn any Shorts ad revenue (it stays in the program and keeps long-form earnings below that). English Shorts pay about $0.03–$0.15 per 1,000 views, and the 20M-view route is impractical. **Use Shorts as a funnel into long-form videos.** Whether Shorts watch time counts toward the 8,000 hours is unconfirmed (it usually doesn't).

### The July 2026 "inauthentic content" clarification
AI is allowed. What loses monetization:
1. Generic, repetitive or template-based content (the same format with only names or plot details swapped).
2. "Off-putting or distressing" content made to provoke emotion.
3. AI personas giving advice on finance, health or legal topics.

YouTube judges the channel as a whole, and publishes no threshold. A rejected channel can reapply after 90 days. Dates in coverage vary (July 15 vs 16). Checked against sources in section 5: the categories are confirmed and the rules themselves are not new.

### What got hit in 2026
- **January 2026:** about 16–18 large faceless channels were removed, including AI biblical-story and AI cartoon-episode channels with about 5.8–5.9M subscribers each.
- **Late 2025:** two AI fake-movie-trailer channels (over 2M subs) were demonetized, then terminated for spam and misleading metadata.
- **Pattern that triggers action:** one template, several uploads a day, scripts shared across channels, and misleading titles or thumbnails. Being faceless is not itself the trigger.
- Kids and religious-story farms were the hardest hit.

### Other points
- Label realistic synthetic footage with the altered/synthetic setting. YouTube says the label alone doesn't hurt reach or monetization.
- Every script needs sources, and AI history and science content is criticized mostly for factual errors.
- Veo-style clips cost about $0.05–0.40 a second through Google's API, so a 10-minute video made only of fresh AI clips would be roughly $30–70 there. Mixing AI clips with stills, maps, loops and charts cuts this a lot, and your web-studio pricing is different from those API rates.

### Format economics
### Long-form: watch hours for the 8,000-hour entry bar (arithmetic, assumed viewing time)
8,000 hours = 480,000 minutes.

| Video | Assumed average watch | Views needed in total |
|---|---|---|
| 10 min | about 4.5 min | about 107,000 |
| 25 min | about 10 min | about 48,000 |
| 1 hour | about 25 min | about 19,000 |
| 2 hours | about 45 min | about 11,000 |

Only the very long formats reach the bar on small view counts, which is why sleep-style history channels exist and why they are crowded and policy-sensitive.

### Shorts: the 10M-views-per-90-days rule
From Feb 1, 2027 a channel needs 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days (about 3.33M a month) to earn any Shorts ad revenue. YouTube has named new incentive programs for channels below that line (Shopping bonuses, brand-deal incentives, trend bonuses) but has published no amounts, eligibility rules or dates, so nothing can be planned on them.

| Median views per Short | Shorts needed per month |
|---|---|
| 4M | about 1 |
| 400K | about 8 |
| 100K | about 33 |
| 20K | about 170 |
| 5K | about 670 |

Below roughly 20K median views the rule is out of reach at any realistic upload pace. Shorts RPM is also low: third-party estimates range from $0.02 to $0.30 per 1,000 views for most niches, with large country differences. These are blog estimates, not YouTube figures.

**Implication:** long-form is the route to the 8,000 hours and to real income. Shorts are a reach experiment.

### 5.4 Ranking: first pass (reasoning-based)

1. **Science and space explainers** built on concepts that can't be filmed (deep time, extreme physics, "how a thing works", engineering failures). AI visuals are a feature here because they illustrate ideas instead of pretending to be footage. "What if" videos fit as a series branch.
2. **Mythology and folklore explained**, comparing the primary texts and how scholars read them, rather than retelling stories. Stylized visuals hide AI flaws, but RPM is lower and retelling-style competition is heavy.
3. **Narrative ancient history**, showing landscapes, objects and maps and never realistic human faces. The "sleep history" slop around it is a reputation risk, so it needs real citations and a distinct angle.

**Honest caveat:** no verified case study of a Veo-era AI-visual science, history or mythology channel with named subscriber and RPM figures turned up. The ranking comes from reasoning about fit and policy risk. Test with 10–15 videos and watch retention before committing.

### 5.5 Recommendations: format study (evidence-based)

1. **History documentary, 15-35 min.** Strong medians on mid-size channels and a small-channel proof (History Hustle). Veo-style clips suit atmosphere and reenactment; use maps and sourced art for facts.
2. **Mysteries, folklore and horror narration, 15-45 min.** The best small-channel evidence of any niche (Ghost Theory, MysteryTV). Avoid real victims and fabricated cases.
3. **Space and science explainers, 10-25 min.** Best match to AI visuals and strong at the top, but **no small-channel proof was found**, so test it in a pilot. **This tempers my earlier ranking, which put science first.**
4. **Economics, geopolitics, company and industry history, 10-25 min.** Strong medians, but mostly maps and data graphics, so AI clips add little.
5. **Sleep and long-listen history, 1-3 h.** Best watch hours per view and Sleepless Historian proves demand, but clones failed and it is the format most exposed to the "repetitive" policy bucket.

1. **Curiosity and "what if" animation with a distinct visual identity** (the Zack D. Films pattern).
2. **Original-character AI series** (the Neural Viz pattern): characters people follow, not generic clips.
3. **Space and science snippets** as a funnel to long-form.
4. History and horror only as cliffhanger trailers for a long-form video.

Put most effort into long-form, which is the route to the watch-hours bar and to real income. Treat Shorts as separate products with their own success numbers, not as a funnel. Do not plan around the Shorts income threshold.

### 5.6 English niches to avoid

- **Kids / bedtime:** hit in the January 2026 wave, plus loss of personalized ads (reported as an 80% revenue cut) and heavy scrutiny.
- **"Boring history" / sleep-history mass format:** the template is the problem.
- **Finance, health or legal advice** from a synthetic persona.
- **True crime about real people:** defamation and "distressing" risk.
- **Religious-story farms**, fake trailers, fan "concepts" and misleading thumbnails.
- **Reddit-style horror and quote-motivation channels:** templated by nature.
- **Animal rescue / "in distress" content:** named in the policy.
- **Real-event geopolitics** with realistic synthetic footage.
- **Daily-upload automation**, shared scripts across channels, or bursts of near-identical Shorts.

## 6. Urdu and Hindi niches

### 6.1 Money and audience

- **Pakistan:** CPM about $0.18–0.90, so RPM roughly $0.10–0.38. Shorts RPM is roughly PKR 8–120 per 1,000 views.
- **India:** RPM about ₹15–170 per 1,000 views overall. Finance runs ₹80–250, tech ₹75–110, education ₹65–92, entertainment ₹8–25, Shorts ₹5–30. English earns 2–3x (or more) more than Hindi in the same niche.
- **Gulf, UK and US diaspora:** CPM from about $4 (Saudi) to $15–36 (US). A diaspora-heavy audience is the main lever for raising Urdu earnings.
- **Where you get paid matters:** YPP eligibility and payout depend on the country of the AdSense account holder, so the choice of company or residency for payments is a later decision. YouTube's official availability page lists the supported countries.
- All figures here are third-party estimates. Realistically, even after monetization, income at these rates is small for a long time.

### 6.2 What is actually working

- **Bandar Apna Dost:** an Indian AI monkey-video channel reported as the most-viewed AI channel worldwide (about $4.25M estimated earnings). Pure entertainment outlier.
- **SA Religious Voice:** Urdu/Hindi Islamic storytelling with AI images. Its top video now shows about 1.6M views against a recent median of about 6,400 (roughly 250x), so it is a one-off hit.
- **Saira Urdu Tales:** AI-animated village stories with 74 subscribers across 35 videos, showing the format alone doesn't guarantee traction.
- **No verified sub or view counts** were found for any large faceless Urdu or Hindi channel in the history, science or story niches. Treat claims from freelance gigs and "best automation channels" blogs as marketing.

### 6.5 Ranking: first pass

1. **South Asian history and "untold stories" in Urdu** (Hindi later). It combines low policy risk, strong AI-visual fit (maps, period scenes, reconstructions) and clearly distinct, researched topics. Main risks: factual accuracy (every script needs citations) and politically sensitive subjects.
2. **Science explainers in Urdu or Hindi.** Excellent visual fit, low policy risk, thinner Urdu competition. Weakness: audience size.

Finance has the highest RPM but is not recommended first: the policy names finance and health for synthetic personas, scam risk is high, and "earn online" is the most saturated area.

2. **Cricket analysis with original graphics and no match footage.**
3. **Kids and moral stories:** medium, with policy and quality risk.

### 6.7 Language and voice

- **Urdu vs Hindi:** the spoken languages are largely mutually intelligible; the difference is vocabulary and script. Hindi has more volume and much more competition, Urdu less competition and less direct monetization (diaspora is the upside). A shared "Hindustani" register reaching both is a hypothesis, not tested.
- **Do not translate one script across both languages.** That reads as repetitive content.
- **Captions:** Hindi uses Devanagari. For Urdu, Roman Urdu is common in search, but no hard data on the split was found. Nastaliq rendering in caption tools is a known weak point, so test your caption renderer first.
- **Urdu TTS:** ElevenLabs offers Urdu with a Pakistani accent, but no independent quality review was found. Run blind listening tests with native speakers.
- **Hindi TTS:** Sarvam Bulbul V3 claims to beat ElevenLabs in its own test and costs less, but doesn't appear to cover Urdu (unverified).
- **Native speech in Veo clips:** no evidence of Hindi or Urdu dialogue support was found. Plan on TTS narration, with clips silent or ambient only, and test before relying on spoken dialogue in clips.

### 6.8 Urdu and Hindi niches to avoid

- **Depicting prophets or sahaba, or Quran recitation, with AI faces and voices.** Many viewers find it offensive, and AI religious content is already under scrutiny.
- **Kids' moral-story cartoons.** No personalized ads, advocacy pressure to ban AI kids content, and templated plots match the repetitive-content definition.
- **Generic mythology story mills** and **motivational quote Shorts.**
- **Real-person likenesses** (Bollywood, Lollywood, politicians): deepfake, defamation and copyright risk.
- **Finance or health advice from a synthetic persona.**
- **India–Pakistan geopolitics and Kashmir:** poor ad suitability, high abuse risk.
- **AI-styled true crime about real victims.**

## 8. Reality check (verified 2026-11-21)

I opened the primary sources and the channels themselves. What held up and what did not:

**Confirmed from primary sources**
- YouTube's own blog confirms the Feb 1, 2027 entry bar: 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days, plus 1,000 subscribers. It also adds a rule the Urdu pass had flagged as unverified: **Shorts ad revenue needs 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days for every channel.** Below that, Shorts earn nothing and long-form earnings continue.
- YouTube's help page lists the "inauthentic content" policy and names "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" as ineligible. It also names using AI "to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented" or to "generate a unique background visual" as acceptable.
- Tubefilter (read directly) confirms the three categories: generic or repetitive, unsatisfying or off-putting, and AI personas on sensitive topics. Tubefilter and Metricool both say this is a clarification and the rules did not change. The help page's changelog shows the rename to "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025, so "July 2026" in earlier notes refers to the clarification, not a new rule.
- YouTube's official availability page lists the countries where YPP sign-up is open. Eligibility and payout follow the country of the AdSense account holder, so which company or residency receives the payments is still your choice to make.
- Removed channels: CuentosFaciantes, ImperiodeJesus and KH Studio return 404. The Screen Culture handle now reads "Handle for sale". This matches the termination reports.

**Corrected or not confirmed**
- Three Minute Wisdom: I could not find its handle, and the only article found says most of its content was wiped. Whether it was demonetized or terminated is unverified.
- Doctor NOS has 2.46M subscribers, not the 1.7M in some reports.
- MagnatesMedia (1.86M subs) was cited as a survivor, but its last upload was about a year ago.
- SA Religious Voice's hit is about 250x its recent median, not 7,000x.

**What real numbers look like**
- Large subscriber counts can hide dead audiences. HINDU STORIES has 1.18M subscribers but a median of 4.6K views. Unknown Mysteries Hindi has 4.96M subscribers but a median of 6.6K. Urdu Facts HD has a median of 1.7K and has been quiet for four months.
- The strongest examples publish rarely and put real work into each video. Fall of Civilizations has 47 videos in five years with a 2.2M median. Kurzgesagt has 401 videos with a 5.65M median.
- Urdu history is real but small. Urdu Diary (1.21M subs) posts almost daily at a 46.5K median. At the Pakistan RPM range in section 4 ($0.10-0.38 per 1,000 views), that is roughly $5-18 per video (my arithmetic, not a sourced figure).
- Sleepless Historian (AI-made, 718K subs) uploads about daily, with 2h+ videos at a 23K median. Very long videos build watch hours quickly, but this is the templated format the policy targets.
- Almost every successful example above is human-produced. No verified new AI-visual channel with strong views turned up in any niche, so the pilot is an experiment.

## 9. Niches to avoid: format study

- **Fabricated or lightly sourced true crime.** Reported removals and policy exposure.
- **AI-persona advice on finance, health or legal topics.** Ineligible under YouTube's policy.
- **Generic listicles and "top 10 facts".** Collapsed in the data.
- **War, news or political content built from AI imagery.** Reported takedowns; accuracy risk.
- **Pure ambient loops.** Low views per video and the lowest RPM.
- **Generic motivation and quote Shorts.** Ratios 0.002-0.012 even for big channels.
- **Sports and news clip compilations.** Copyright and tiny ratios.
- **Sacred figures (prophets) in AI imagery.** Widely treated as unacceptable; needs human review.
- **Kids and nursery AI content.** Quality scrutiny and possible made-for-kids limits (not verified).
- **Pure AI-animal slop.** Bimodal results plus the highest enforcement risk.
- **Fast-news AI and tech coverage.** An automated pipeline cannot keep pace.

## 10. Implications for the plan and next steps

1. Treat **long-form and Shorts as separate products**, each with its own topics, success numbers and (possibly) its own channel. Do not assume Shorts feed long-form.
2. Put the pilot on **one long-form niche first**, with Shorts as a small side test. Candidate pilots: history documentary, or mysteries/folklore, because they have small-channel proof. Space and science stays a candidate but needs the pilot to show it works.
3. Use AI clips where the niche allows (about 50-60% for space, mysteries and history atmosphere; about 15-25% for economics) and fill the rest with maps, stills, charts and archival art.
4. Build the plan's `clip_type` and per-niche AI-clip share into the scene-plan template.
5. Check the Shorts income threshold before investing in any Shorts-led niche.

### Next steps
1. **Decide the first niche** (history documentary, mysteries and folklore, or space and science), or run a short pilot of two.
2. **Pilot:** 10-15 long-form videos in review-publish mode, with varied structures, a source for every claim, and retention checked before scaling. Shorts only as a separate, small experiment.
3. **Close the gaps cheaply:** pull real stats for 5-10 small channels in the chosen niche (vidIQ or OutlierKit); run a native-speaker blind test of Urdu and Hindi TTS; test whether Veoframe or Veo produces Hindi or Urdu speech; collect first-hand creator accounts of AI-channel monetization (Reddit was inaccessible to my tools).
4. **Feed the winner into the pipeline plan** (`AI Video Channel Plan`): channel style guide, language, topic queue seeded with the ideas above.

#### 2. Rules that apply to every niche

##### Monetization thresholds
- From **Feb 1, 2027**, new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus **8,000 public watch hours** (last 365 days) or **20M Shorts views** (last 90 days). Today it is 4,000 hours or 10M Shorts views. Existing partners are exempt. Confidence: high (several outlets agree); the YouTube page itself was not read.
- A brand-new channel will not reach 4,000 hours in the roughly 10 weeks left, so plan for the **8,000-hour** bar.
- At a 5-minute average view, 8,000 hours is about **96,000 long-form views** (about 120,000 at 4 minutes).
- Shorts are a poor revenue path. Confirmed on YouTube's own blog: from Feb 1, 2027 a channel needs 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days to earn any Shorts ad revenue (it stays in the program and keeps long-form earnings below that). English Shorts pay about $0.03–$0.15 per 1,000 views, and the 20M-view route is impractical. **Use Shorts as a funnel into long-form videos.** Whether Shorts watch time counts toward the 8,000 hours is unconfirmed (it usually doesn't).

##### The July 2026 "inauthentic content" clarification
AI is allowed. What loses monetization:
1. Generic, repetitive or template-based content (the same format with only names or plot details swapped).
2. "Off-putting or distressing" content made to provoke emotion.
3. AI personas giving advice on finance, health or legal topics.

YouTube judges the channel as a whole, and publishes no threshold. A rejected channel can reapply after 90 days. Dates in coverage vary (July 15 vs 16). Checked against sources in section 5: the categories are confirmed and the rules themselves are not new.

##### What got hit in 2026
- **January 2026:** about 16–18 large faceless channels were removed, including AI biblical-story and AI cartoon-episode channels with about 5.8–5.9M subscribers each.
- **Late 2025:** two AI fake-movie-trailer channels (over 2M subs) were demonetized, then terminated for spam and misleading metadata.
- **Pattern that triggers action:** one template, several uploads a day, scripts shared across channels, and misleading titles or thumbnails. Being faceless is not itself the trigger.
- Kids and religious-story farms were the hardest hit.

##### Other points
- Label realistic synthetic footage with the altered/synthetic setting. YouTube says the label alone doesn't hurt reach or monetization.
- Every script needs sources, and AI history and science content is criticized mostly for factual errors.
- Veo-style clips cost about $0.05–0.40 a second through Google's API, so a 10-minute video made only of fresh AI clips would be roughly $30–70 there. Mixing AI clips with stills, maps, loops and charts cuts this a lot, and your web-studio pricing is different from those API rates.

#### 5. Reality check (verified 2026-11-21)

I opened the primary sources and the channels themselves. What held up and what did not:

**Confirmed from primary sources**
- YouTube's own blog confirms the Feb 1, 2027 entry bar: 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days, plus 1,000 subscribers. It also adds a rule the Urdu pass had flagged as unverified: **Shorts ad revenue needs 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days for every channel.** Below that, Shorts earn nothing and long-form earnings continue.
- YouTube's help page lists the "inauthentic content" policy and names "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" as ineligible. It also names using AI "to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented" or to "generate a unique background visual" as acceptable.
- Tubefilter (read directly) confirms the three categories: generic or repetitive, unsatisfying or off-putting, and AI personas on sensitive topics. Tubefilter and Metricool both say this is a clarification and the rules did not change. The help page's changelog shows the rename to "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025, so "July 2026" in earlier notes refers to the clarification, not a new rule.
- YouTube's official availability page lists the countries where YPP sign-up is open. Eligibility and payout follow the country of the AdSense account holder, so which company or residency receives the payments is still your choice to make.
- Removed channels: CuentosFaciantes, ImperiodeJesus and KH Studio return 404. The Screen Culture handle now reads "Handle for sale". This matches the termination reports.

**Corrected or not confirmed**
- Three Minute Wisdom: I could not find its handle, and the only article found says most of its content was wiped. Whether it was demonetized or terminated is unverified.
- Doctor NOS has 2.46M subscribers, not the 1.7M in some reports.
- MagnatesMedia (1.86M subs) was cited as a survivor, but its last upload was about a year ago.
- SA Religious Voice's hit is about 250x its recent median, not 7,000x.

**What real numbers look like**
- Large subscriber counts can hide dead audiences. HINDU STORIES has 1.18M subscribers but a median of 4.6K views. Unknown Mysteries Hindi has 4.96M subscribers but a median of 6.6K. Urdu Facts HD has a median of 1.7K and has been quiet for four months.
- The strongest examples publish rarely and put real work into each video. Fall of Civilizations has 47 videos in five years with a 2.2M median. Kurzgesagt has 401 videos with a 5.65M median.
- Urdu history is real but small. Urdu Diary (1.21M subs) posts almost daily at a 46.5K median. At the Pakistan RPM range in section 4 ($0.10-0.38 per 1,000 views), that is roughly $5-18 per video (my arithmetic, not a sourced figure).
- Sleepless Historian (AI-made, 718K subs) uploads about daily, with 2h+ videos at a 23K median. Very long videos build watch hours quickly, but this is the templated format the policy targets.
- Almost every successful example above is human-produced. No verified new AI-visual channel with strong views turned up in any niche, so the pilot is an experiment.

1. **Decide the first channel:** English science/space (recommended) or Urdu history.
2. **Close the verification gaps** (cheap, before building):
   - Read YouTube's own Help pages for the inauthentic-content wording, the country list and the Shorts thresholds.
   - Run a native-speaker blind test of Urdu and Hindi TTS.
   - Test whether Veoframe/Veo produces Hindi or Urdu speech.
   - Pull real stats for 5–10 small channels in the chosen niche (vidIQ or OutlierKit).
3. **Pilot:** 10–15 videos in review-publish mode, with a mix of formats, sources cited in every description, and retention checked before scaling.
4. **Feed the winner into the pipeline plan** (`AI Video Channel Plan.md`): channel style guide, language, topic queue seeded with the 15 ideas above.

### Appendix B: Niche research by format: which niches work in long-form, in Shorts, or both

Date: 2026-11-21. Two research passes (long-form and Shorts), plus my own measurements. English and Urdu/Hindi are kept separate. This builds on `Niche Research - AI Video Channel.md` and `How Viral Videos Are Made.md`.

#### How to read this

- **Measured** numbers come from a script that reads public YouTube channel pages on 2026-11-21: subscribers, the median and maximum views of the latest 20 long-form uploads, and the median and maximum views of the latest ~30 Shorts. About 250 distinct channels were tried; roughly 40% of guessed handles did not exist, so the sample is whatever could be found and measured.
- **Cross-check:** at least eight channels measured by both research passes and by me gave identical numbers (for example Mr. Nightmare 993K long / 289K Shorts, Kurzgesagt 5.65M / 1.4M, Urdu Diary 46.5K, Sleepless Historian 23K). That confirms the tool is consistent. It does not confirm every other number, which I have not re-checked one by one.
- **"Ratio"** = Shorts median divided by subscribers. A high ratio means Shorts reach people beyond the existing audience.
- **Big warning, survivorship bias:** the channels that could be found are mostly large and successful. Evidence on small or new channels is thin. Where a niche has no small-channel proof, this document says so.
- **Format (AI-made vs human-made)** could not be measured. Most labels are the researchers' reading of the channel, so treat them as unknown unless a source is named.
- Revenue and average view duration are not visible to the script. Watch-hour and RPM figures are arithmetic on stated assumptions or blog estimates.

| Hindi/Urdu horror and story narration | Strong | Weak | **Long only** | Horror Stories Hindi Urdu 699K long, no Shorts; Hindi Horror Stories 46K vs 8.8K |
| Urdu/Hindi history and explainers | Medium-strong | none or weak | **Long** | Urdu Diary 46.5K median; Dekho Suno Jano 133K |
| Hindi mythology | Weak | Weak | **Neither**, as measured | HINDU STORIES 1.18M subs, 4.6K median; a small channel at 40 views |

**What this says:**
- The niches that win in long-form (explainers, documentary, mystery) are mostly weak in Shorts, and the niches that win in Shorts (characters, cute animals, quick curiosity) are mostly weak in long-form. A few large, polished channels win in both.
- This matches YouTube's own statement that viewers rarely follow a creator across formats.

#### 2. Format economics

##### Long-form: watch hours for the 8,000-hour entry bar (arithmetic, assumed viewing time)
8,000 hours = 480,000 minutes.

| Video | Assumed average watch | Views needed in total |
|---|---|---|
| 10 min | about 4.5 min | about 107,000 |
| 25 min | about 10 min | about 48,000 |
| 1 hour | about 25 min | about 19,000 |
| 2 hours | about 45 min | about 11,000 |

Only the very long formats reach the bar on small view counts, which is why sleep-style history channels exist and why they are crowded and policy-sensitive.

##### Shorts: the 10M-views-per-90-days rule
From Feb 1, 2027 a channel needs 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days (about 3.33M a month) to earn any Shorts ad revenue. YouTube has named new incentive programs for channels below that line (Shopping bonuses, brand-deal incentives, trend bonuses) but has published no amounts, eligibility rules or dates, so nothing can be planned on them.

| Median views per Short | Shorts needed per month |
|---|---|
| 4M | about 1 |
| 400K | about 8 |
| 100K | about 33 |
| 20K | about 170 |
| 5K | about 670 |

Below roughly 20K median views the rule is out of reach at any realistic upload pace. Shorts RPM is also low: third-party estimates range from $0.02 to $0.30 per 1,000 views for most niches, with large country differences. These are blog estimates, not YouTube figures.

**Implication:** long-form is the route to the 8,000 hours and to real income. Shorts are a reach experiment.

#### 6. Recommendations

##### English: long-form core (ranked)
1. **History documentary, 15-35 min.** Strong medians on mid-size channels and a small-channel proof (History Hustle). Veo-style clips suit atmosphere and reenactment; use maps and sourced art for facts.
2. **Mysteries, folklore and horror narration, 15-45 min.** The best small-channel evidence of any niche (Ghost Theory, MysteryTV). Avoid real victims and fabricated cases.
3. **Space and science explainers, 10-25 min.** Best match to AI visuals and strong at the top, but **no small-channel proof was found**, so test it in a pilot. **This tempers my earlier ranking, which put science first.**
4. **Economics, geopolitics, company and industry history, 10-25 min.** Strong medians, but mostly maps and data graphics, so AI clips add little.
5. **Sleep and long-listen history, 1-3 h.** Best watch hours per view and Sleepless Historian proves demand, but clones failed and it is the format most exposed to the "repetitive" policy bucket.

##### English: Shorts (separate experiments)
1. **Curiosity and "what if" animation with a distinct visual identity** (the Zack D. Films pattern).
2. **Original-character AI series** (the Neural Viz pattern): characters people follow, not generic clips.
3. **Space and science snippets** as a funnel to long-form.
4. History and horror only as cliffhanger trailers for a long-form video.

##### Urdu/Hindi: long-form first (ranked)
1. **Urdu South Asian and political-history explainers, 15-40 min** (Urdu Diary is the verified proof point).
2. **Urdu or Hindi horror and story narration, 20-60 min** (Urdu Corner, Hindi Horror Stories).
3. **Hindi or Urdu science and general-knowledge explainers, 10-25 min** (SciMyth), with no small-channel proof.
4. **Hindi company and business-story explainers** (no advice).
5. **Geopolitics explainers:** demand is plausible but I found no measured channel, and political content carries local risk.

##### Urdu/Hindi: Shorts (experiments)
1. **Original-character comedy or stories:** the strongest results and the highest policy exposure; only viable with real scripts and distinct characters.
2. **Cricket analysis with original graphics and no match footage.**
3. **Kids and moral stories:** medium, with policy and quality risk.

##### Rule of thumb for effort
Put most effort into long-form, which is the route to the watch-hours bar and to real income. Treat Shorts as separate products with their own success numbers, not as a funnel. Do not plan around the Shorts income threshold.

#### 8. Niches to avoid
- **Fabricated or lightly sourced true crime.** Reported removals and policy exposure.
- **AI-persona advice on finance, health or legal topics.** Ineligible under YouTube's policy.
- **Generic listicles and "top 10 facts".** Collapsed in the data.
- **War, news or political content built from AI imagery.** Reported takedowns; accuracy risk.
- **Pure ambient loops.** Low views per video and the lowest RPM.
- **Generic motivation and quote Shorts.** Ratios 0.002-0.012 even for big channels.
- **Sports and news clip compilations.** Copyright and tiny ratios.
- **Sacred figures (prophets) in AI imagery.** Widely treated as unacceptable; needs human review.
- **Kids and nursery AI content.** Quality scrutiny and possible made-for-kids limits (not verified).
- **Pure AI-animal slop.** Bimodal results plus the highest enforcement risk.
- **Fast-news AI and tech coverage.** An automated pipeline cannot keep pace.

#### 9. Implications for the plan (not yet applied)
1. Treat **long-form and Shorts as separate products**, each with its own topics, success numbers and (possibly) its own channel. Do not assume Shorts feed long-form.
2. Put the pilot on **one long-form niche first**, with Shorts as a small side test. Candidate pilots: history documentary, or mysteries/folklore, because they have small-channel proof. Space and science stays a candidate but needs the pilot to show it works.
3. Use AI clips where the niche allows (about 50-60% for space, mysteries and history atmosphere; about 15-25% for economics) and fill the rest with maps, stills, charts and archival art.
4. Build the plan's `clip_type` and per-niche AI-clip share into the scene-plan template.
5. Check the Shorts income threshold before investing in any Shorts-led niche.
